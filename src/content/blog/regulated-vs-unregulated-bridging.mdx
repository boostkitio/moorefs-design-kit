Bridging loans come in two kinds: regulated and unregulated. The difference has nothing to do with the lender and everything to do with the property and who lives in it.

## The short version

- **Regulated:** the loan is secured on your home, or a home that you or a close family member will live in. The Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) regulates it, with consumer protections.
- **Unregulated:** the loan is secured on investment or business property, such as a buy to let, a commercial building, land or a property bought to develop. Most bridging for business and investment purposes falls here.

## When a bridging loan is regulated

Under the FCA's rules, a loan is a regulated mortgage when all of these are true:

1. you borrow as an individual (or as a trustee), not as a company
2. the loan is secured on property in the UK
3. at least 40% of that property is used, or will be used, as a home by you or a close family member

A regulated bridging loan typically runs for 12 months or less. A classic example is buying your next home before you have sold your current one.

## When it is unregulated

If any of those tests is not met, the loan is usually unregulated. Common examples:

- buying at auction to refurbish and sell or let
- buying a commercial or semi-commercial building as an investment
- bridging through a limited company
- funding a development or conversion

Unregulated bridging can sometimes run longer than 12 months and is assessed more on the property and your exit plan than on your personal income.

## Why it matters to you

A regulated loan comes with protections: lenders and advisers must follow FCA rules on affordability and how they treat you, and you can complain to the Financial Ombudsman Service if things go wrong. With unregulated lending most of those protections do not apply, so it matters even more that you understand the costs and have a realistic plan to repay.

## What every bridge needs: an exit

Bridging is short-term borrowing with interest charged monthly, so it is far more expensive than a standard mortgage over a year. Before you commit, you need a clear way to repay it, usually:

- **selling the property,** or
- **refinancing** onto a longer-term mortgage once the work is done or the property is let

I check the exit is realistic before you go ahead, because a bridge that runs past its end date gets expensive quickly.
