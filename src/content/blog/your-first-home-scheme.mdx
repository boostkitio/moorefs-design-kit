Saving a deposit is the hardest part of buying a first home for most people I speak to. The monthly repayments are often affordable; it is the lump sum that takes years. The government's new **Your First Home** scheme, confirmed in the Budget on 28 October 2026, is aimed squarely at that problem.

## How it works

You buy a new-build home in England from a developer signed up to the scheme, and pay for it in three parts:

- **your deposit:** at least 2.5% of the price
- **a government equity loan:** 20% of the price, interest free for the first five years
- **a repayment mortgage:** the remaining 77.5%, from a lender taking part in the scheme

You own the home outright from the day you complete. The government is not a joint owner or your landlord.

Here is GOV.UK's own example for a £300,000 home:

| | With Your First Home | With a 95% mortgage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Your deposit | £7,500 | £15,000 |
| Equity loan | £60,000 | None |
| Mortgage | £232,500 | £285,000 |

Because the mortgage is smaller, the monthly repayment is lower too, at least for the first five years.

## Who can use it

You will normally need to:

- be a first-time buyer
- buy a new-build home in England, under the price cap for the area
- live in it as your main home
- have a household income of £110,000 or less outside London, or £140,000 or less in London
- pass a participating lender's affordability and credit checks

The price cap is £650,000 in inner London and £500,000 in outer London. Caps for every other area, including Kent and the rest of the South East, are due to be published shortly. Reading's is £375,000, which gives an idea of the level outside London.

## What it costs after year five

The equity loan is not free money, and this is the part people most often miss. From year six you pay interest on it, separately from your mortgage:

| Period | Interest on the equity loan |
| --- | --- |
| Years 1 to 5 | 0% |
| Year 6 | 1.5% |
| Year 7 onwards | 3% |

On a £60,000 loan, GOV.UK puts that at about £75 a month in year six and £150 a month from year seven. Those payments do not reduce what you owe.

When you sell, or choose to repay, you pay back 20% of what your home is worth at that time, not 20% of what you paid. If your home rises in value, you repay more. If it falls, you repay less.

Most people using a scheme like this plan to remortgage onto a bigger loan, or pay off part of the equity loan, within the first five years. It is worth planning for that from the start rather than when the interest begins.

## Is it right for you?

It can be a good route if you have a steady income but have not been able to save a large deposit, and you like the new-build homes available in your area. It is less suitable if:

- you want an older home, as only new builds qualify
- you might move again within a few years, because new builds can sell for less than their brand-new price
- your budget only works while the equity loan is interest free

New builds also come with their own costs, such as service and estate charges, so include those when you work out what you can afford.

## Saving the 2.5%

The Budget also changed the rules on saving for a first home:

- If you have a **Lifetime ISA** and the home costs more than £450,000, the withdrawal charge has dropped from 25% to 20%. In practice you get back roughly what you paid in, but lose the government bonus.
- A new **First Time Buyer ISA** arrives in April 2029, with up to £4,000 a year, a 25% government bonus for first homes up to £450,000 and no upper age limit.

## What to do now

Pre-registration opens by the end of December 2026, applications from February 2027, and the first purchases are expected in April 2027. Until then, a few things put you in a stronger position:

1. Find out what you could borrow on a 77.5% mortgage, and whether the monthly cost still works when equity loan interest starts. My [mortgage repayment calculator](/calculators/mortgage-repayment) helps with a first look.
2. Check your credit file and keep your bank statements tidy, as lenders will look at both.
3. Look at which developers near you are signing up once the list is published.
