Walk down almost any high street in Kent or South London and you will see them: shops with flats above, offices with apartments on the upper floors, pubs with living space. Lenders call these semi-commercial or mixed-use properties, and they sit between residential and commercial lending.

The single most important detail is how much of the building is residential, and who lives in it.

## The 40% rule

Under the Financial Conduct Authority's rules, a mortgage is regulated when you borrow as an individual and at least 40% of the property is used, or will be used, as a home. The 40% is measured by floor area, adding up every storey, not by value.

In practice:

- **You or a family member will live in the flat, and it is at least 40% of the building.** The mortgage is likely to be regulated, with the consumer protections that brings.
- **The flat is let to tenants and you are buying as an investment.** The mortgage is usually unregulated, even if the flat is more than 40% of the building, because you are borrowing for business purposes.
- **You are buying through a limited company.** The mortgage is unregulated.
- **The residential part is less than 40%.** The mortgage is unregulated.

These rules have exceptions, so treat this as a guide. I confirm which applies to your purchase before we go ahead.

## Why lenders like semi-commercial property

Because part of the building is residential, many lenders see semi-commercial property as lower risk than a purely commercial building. That can mean:

- more lenders to choose from
- pricing that is often better than fully commercial mortgages
- the rent from both the shop and the flat counting towards affordability

It also means valuers look at both parts. A shop on a long lease with a flat let on an assured shorthold tenancy is easy for a lender to understand. A vacant shop or a flat that does not meet residential standards is harder.

## Things to check before you buy

- **Access to the flat.** A separate entrance from the street is usually preferred to access through the shop.
- **The commercial lease.** Length, rent reviews and the tenant's strength all matter.
- **Planning use.** Make sure both parts have the right planning use for how you plan to use them.
- **Condition.** Flats above shops are sometimes tired. If work is needed before a mortgage lender will accept it, a refurbishment bridging loan can fund it first.

## Running the shop yourself

If your business will trade from the ground floor and you will let the flat, the lender will look at your business accounts and the residential rent together. Some lenders are comfortable with that mix and some are not, which is where advice helps.
