If you own rental property in your own name, the Budget on 28 October 2026 will raise your tax bill. From 6 April 2027, rental profit in England gets its own tax rates, separate from the rates on your salary, and they are higher than first planned.

## The new rates

| Your tax band | Rate on rental profit now | From April 2027 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basic rate | 20% | 24% |
| Higher rate | 40% | 44% |
| Additional rate | 45% | 49% |

Last year's Budget had already announced a two percentage point rise from April 2027. This Budget adds another two points from the same date, so the total rise is four points. Your salary, pension and other income still decide which band your rental profit falls into.

## What it could cost you

As a simple example, take £10,000 of rental profit a year:

- a **basic-rate** taxpayer pays £2,400 instead of £2,000, which is £400 more a year
- a **higher-rate** taxpayer pays £4,400 instead of £4,000, which is £400 more a year

If you have a mortgage, the calculation is different. Individual landlords cannot deduct mortgage interest from their rent; instead they get a tax credit, which has been given at the basic rate. Ask your accountant to run your own figures, as the result depends on your whole income and borrowing.

## What it means for your buy to let mortgage

The tax change does not affect the mortgage you already have. It does matter when you next borrow or remortgage, for three reasons.

**Your profit margin gets thinner.** Higher tax leaves less of the rent in your pocket, so it is worth checking that each property still pays its way, particularly one coming off a fixed rate soon.

**Lenders already treat higher-rate taxpayers differently.** When a lender works out how much it will lend on a buy to let, it checks that the rent covers the mortgage interest by a set margin. Most lenders ask for a bigger margin from higher-rate taxpayers than from basic-rate taxpayers or limited companies. My [buy to let calculator](/calculators/buy-to-let) shows how that works.

**Limited companies are taxed differently.** Rent received by a limited company is taxed under corporation tax, not these new rates, though money you take out of the company is taxed separately. Many lenders offer buy to let mortgages to limited companies. Moving properties you already own into a company usually counts as a sale, which can mean stamp duty and capital gains tax, so take tax advice before you decide. I can then tell you how lenders would view either route.

## The rental market

Higher costs may push some landlords to sell, which could mean fewer homes to let while tenant demand stays high. That is a reason to review your portfolio, not to panic: the right decision depends on your properties, your borrowing and your plans.

## What to do now

Before April 2027:

1. Ask your accountant what the new rates mean for your tax bill.
2. Check when each of your fixed rates ends, and whether the rent still covers the mortgage comfortably at today's rates.
