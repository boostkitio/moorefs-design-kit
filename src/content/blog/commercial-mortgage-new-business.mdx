Yes, you can get a commercial mortgage for a new business, but it is harder than for an established one. With no long trading record to look at, a lender has to judge you, your experience and your plan instead. Some lenders will not consider a business under two or three years old. Others will, and that is where a broker earns their keep.

## Why lenders are more cautious

For premises your own business will trade from, a lender's main question is whether the business can afford the repayments. Normally it answers that from two or three years of accounts. A new business cannot show that, so the lender is taking more of a view on the future.

That usually means:

- a **bigger deposit,** often towards the top of the 25% to 40% range or above
- more **questions about you** and your track record
- a **personal guarantee** from the directors
- sometimes a **lower loan** than an established business would get

## What helps a newer business get a yes

**Experience in the trade.** A lender is far more comfortable lending to a chef opening their own restaurant after ten years in kitchens than to someone new to the industry. Set out your experience clearly.

**A realistic business plan.** Include projections for at least the next couple of years, the assumptions behind them, and what happens if sales are slower than hoped. Lenders read a lot of optimistic plans, so honest ones stand out.

**Some trading history, even if short.** Six months of management accounts and bank statements is better than none. If you are buying an existing business along with its premises, the previous owner's accounts can help.

**A larger deposit or extra security.** Putting more of your own money in, or offering another property as additional security, reduces the lender's risk.

**Your personal finances.** Lenders often look at the directors' own income, assets and credit history. A clean credit file and some personal wealth outside the business both help.

## If the timing is not right yet

Sometimes the best answer is to rent for another year or two while the business builds a track record, then buy. Another route is buying the property as an investment through a separate company and letting it to your business, where the lender looks more at the rent and the lease. Whether that works depends on your situation and the tax side, so speak to your accountant as well as me.
