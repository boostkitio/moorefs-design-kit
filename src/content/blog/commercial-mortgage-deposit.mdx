The short answer: most commercial mortgage lenders want a deposit of 25% to 40% of the price. A few lend up to 80% to established businesses buying their own premises, and some cases need more than 40%.

Where you land in that range depends on a handful of things.

## What moves the deposit up or down

| Factor | Usually means a smaller deposit | Usually means a bigger deposit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who uses the building | Your own established business | An investment let to someone else |
| Trading history | Several years of steady profits | A new or loss-making business |
| Type of property | Offices, shops, standard industrial units | Pubs, hotels, care homes, specialist buildings |
| Leases (investment) | Long lease to an established tenant | Short lease, new tenant or empty unit |
| Your experience | Years in the trade or as a landlord | First venture in this sector |

As a rough guide, lenders typically go up to 75% to 80% of the value for owner-occupiers and 70% to 75% for investment property. Those are maximums, not promises: plenty of cases are offered less.

## Can you buy with no deposit?

Not usually from savings alone, but there are ways to reduce the cash you put in:

- **Extra security.** Some lenders will take a charge over another property you own, such as an investment property, in place of some of the deposit.
- **Equity in other property.** You may be able to raise money against property you already own and use it towards the deposit, as long as the overall borrowing is affordable.
- **Investors or partners.** Money from a business partner or shareholder can count, although the lender will want to know where it comes from.

Each of these puts more of your assets at risk, so I will talk through what happens if things do not go to plan before you decide.

## The other costs to budget for

The deposit is only part of the cash you need. Allow for:

- **Stamp duty** at non-residential rates. See [buying commercial property](/blog/buying-commercial-property) for the current bands.
- **The lender's arrangement fee,** often 1% to 2% of the loan. It can usually be added to the loan.
- **A valuation** by a chartered surveyor, paid by you and priced on the property.
- **Legal fees** for your solicitor and, usually, the lender's solicitor.

I set every cost out in writing before you commit, so there are no surprises near completion.
