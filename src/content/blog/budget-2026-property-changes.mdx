The Budget on 28 October 2026 had more in it for buyers, homeowners and landlords than most. None of it changes mortgage rates directly, but several measures change how much deposit some buyers need, how first-time buyers can save, and what letting a property costs. Here is a plain summary of what was announced and who it affects.

## First-time buyers: a 2.5% deposit on new builds

The new **Your First Home** scheme lets first-time buyers in England buy a new-build home with a deposit of 2.5%. The government lends 20% of the price as an equity loan, interest free for five years, and you take a repayment mortgage for the remaining 77.5%.

- Household income must be £110,000 or less outside London, or £140,000 or less in London.
- Each area has a price cap: £650,000 in inner London and £500,000 in outer London, with lower caps elsewhere.
- Pre-registration opens by the end of December 2026, applications from February 2027, and the first purchases are expected from April 2027.

It will not suit everyone, because the equity loan grows with the value of your home and carries interest from year six. I go through the numbers in [Your First Home: how the 2.5% deposit scheme works](/blog/your-first-home-scheme).

## Saving for a first home: a new ISA and a smaller Lifetime ISA penalty

- **Lifetime ISA:** since 29 October 2026, if you use your Lifetime ISA towards a first home costing more than £450,000, the withdrawal charge is 20% instead of 25%. That means you get back roughly what you paid in, but not the government bonus. For homes up to £450,000 there is still no charge.
- **First Time Buyer ISA:** from April 2029 a new account replaces the Lifetime ISA for new savers. You can pay in up to £4,000 a year and the government adds 25% when you use it to buy a first home worth up to £450,000. There is no upper age limit.
- **Cash ISA:** the £20,000 yearly limit stays from April 2027. The cut to £12,000 announced last year will not happen.

If you live or want to buy in London or the South East, the £450,000 cap matters. Many first homes here cost more, so it is worth knowing early whether your ISA will pay out in full.

## Landlords: higher tax on rent from April 2027

From 6 April 2027, rental profit received personally in England will be taxed at 24%, 44% and 49%, depending on your tax band. That is four percentage points above today's rates: last year's Budget had already planned a two point rise, and this Budget added two more. I explain what it means for your buy to let borrowing in [Landlords: tax on rental income rises to 24% from April 2027](/blog/landlord-tax-april-2027).

## Homes worth £2 million or more: a yearly surcharge from April 2028

Owners of homes in England worth £2 million or more will pay a **High Value Council Tax Surcharge** on top of their normal council tax from April 2028. Homes are banded on their value in 2026, and the Valuation Office will carry out a valuation exercise to identify them.

| Value of the home | Yearly surcharge |
| --- | --- |
| £2 million to £2.5 million | £2,500 |
| £2.5 million to £3.5 million | £3,500 |
| £3.5 million to £5 million | £5,000 |
| £5 million or more | £7,500 |

Owners with a household income of £35,000 or less, savings of £16,000 or less, or a disabled member of the household can defer payment until the home changes hands, with interest added. If you are buying near the threshold, factor the surcharge into your monthly budget, because lenders look at your outgoings when they work out what you can borrow.

## What to do now

Most of these changes start in 2027 or later, so nobody needs to rush. If you are planning a purchase, a remortgage or a buy to let in the next year or two, though, the details can change your numbers. I am happy to look at where you stand and talk through your options, with no obligation.
